The 3/8 Scale for Shortest Directed Paths

Result

Let M0,n be the unconditioned KMSW cell encoded by n independent steps, each uniform on {(1, −1), (−1, 0), (0, 1)}. The cell consists of its n + 1 active edges with their endpoint identifications; missing boundary edges are excluded. Let Δn be the maximum, over ordered vertex pairs joined by a directed path inside the cell, of the minimum number of edges in such a path.

There are deterministic constants c, C > 0 such that, for every integer n ≥ 0 and every real A ≥ 1, ℙ[Δn > A(n + 1)3/8] ≤ C e−cA.

For every n ≥ 1 and every median mn of Δn—meaning ℙ[Δn ≤ mn] ≥ 1/2 and ℙ[Δn ≥ mn] ≥ 1/2—one has c n3/8 ≤ mn ≤ C n3/8, with constants independent of n and of the choice of median.

SDP Graph Limits, Theorem 1.2, equations (1.7)–(1.8), p. 3; model and distance definition (1.4), p. 2.

Tightness and Continuous SDP Limits

Result

Let M be the uniform infinite bipolar-oriented triangulation. Write p(e) = (i(e), j(e)) for the times of an edge e in the north- and west-going primal Peano explorations, rooted at the same edge and oriented so both times increase along directed paths. For edges e ≼ f, meaning that a directed path can start with e and end with f, let d•M(e, f) be the minimum number of edges in such a path, with d•M(e, e) = 1. Comparability is equivalent to p(e) ≤ p(f) coordinatewise.

For each n ≥ 1, choose any median mn of the maximal finite vertex-to-vertex SDP distance in an unconditioned n-increment KMSW cell with uniform increments from {(1, −1), (−1, 0), (0, 1)}. Put Un = {p(e)/n : e ∈ E(M)}, Wn = {(x, y) ∈ Un × Un : x ≤ y coordinatewise}, and Dn(p(e)/n, p(f)/n) = d•M(e, f)/mn. Let Γn = {(w, Dn(w)) : w ∈ Wn}.

The laws of (Wn, Γn) are tight in 𝔽(ℝ4) × 𝔽(ℝ5), where 𝔽 denotes closed subsets with the Fell topology. Every sequence n → ∞ therefore has a subsequence converging in law. Every such limit is almost surely (W, {(w, D(w)) : w ∈ W}) for a locally bounded continuous function D : W → [0, ∞): above every point of W there is exactly one finite limiting height. The function is uniquely determined by its limiting graph; uniqueness of the subsequential law is not asserted.

SDP Graph Limits, definitions (1.2)–(1.6) and Theorem 1.1, pp. 2–3; stochastic equicontinuity and local height bounds, Propositions 7.2–7.3, pp. 18–19; graph-limit proof, pp. 19–20.

Nontrivial SDP Limits

Result

On the uniform infinite bipolar-oriented triangulation M, give each edge e its north- and west-going Peano times p(e), and let d•M(e, f) be the shortest directed-path length from first edge e to last edge f, counting both and taking d•M(e, e) = 1. Let Δn be the maximum finite vertex-to-vertex SDP distance in a fresh n-increment KMSW cell with independent uniform steps from {(1, −1), (−1, 0), (0, 1)}, and choose its upper integer median mn = max{m ∈ ℕ : ℙ[Δn ≥ m] ≥ 1/2}. Divide the exploration times by n and the distances by mn.

Set Un = {p(e)/n : e ∈ E(M)}, Wn = {(x, y) ∈ Un × Un : x ≤ y coordinatewise}, Dn(p(e)/n, p(f)/n) = d•M(e, f)/mn, and Γn = Graph(Wn, Dn). Suppose that along a deterministic sequence nr → ∞ these closed domains and graphs converge in law, jointly with the rescaled primal and dual peanosphere data, to (W, Γ), where almost surely Γ = Graph(W, D) for a finite-valued function D : W → [0, ∞).

Then ℙ[there exist x ≠ y with (x, y) ∈ W and D(x, y) > 0] = 1. In the joint realization constructed in the note, almost surely infinitely many pairwise disjoint unit north-Peano cells contain such a positive-distance witness. Thus the limiting function is almost surely not identically zero; the statement does not require, or establish, positive distance for every distinct comparable pair.

The Small-γ LQG Dimension Expansion

Result

Proposed asymptotic. Let dγ denote the almost sure Hausdorff dimension of the planar γ-Liouville quantum gravity metric, with the Gaussian free field normalized to have logarithmic covariance singularity log(1/|z − w|). As γ ↓ 0, the proposed expansion is dγ = 2 + kγ4/3 + o(γ4/3), where k is a deterministic, strictly positive, finite constant.

The coefficient is characterized by a Gaussian variational problem. Let Vn consist of continuous functions on [0, 1] that vanish at the endpoints and are affine on each interval of the uniform mesh 16−n. Let (Zf) be the centered Gaussian family with covariance Cov(Zf, Zg) = π∫01(|f| + |g| − |f − g|) dx, and set an = 𝔼 supf∈Vn{Zf − ½∫01|f′|² dx}. The stated theorem gives 0 < a* = limn→∞ an/n = infn≥1 an/n < ∞ and k = 2−1/3a*/log 16.

Equivalently, the planar LFPP exponent satisfies λ(ξ)/ξ4/3 → a*/log 16 as ξ ↓ 0, with parameter relation ξ = γ/dγ and λ(γ/dγ) = 1 − (2 + γ²/2)/dγ. This specifies a leading coefficient; its numerical value is not evaluated in the note.

05

Gaussian Fluctuations of LQG Distance

Result

Proposed theorem. Let h be a whole-plane Gaussian free field with unit-circle average h1(0) = 0. For each 0 < γ < 2, let Dhγ be the γ-LQG metric, using the same field for all γ. Fix its deterministic scale by requiring the lower median of Cγ = infs,t∈[0,1] Dhγ(is, 1 + it) to equal one. These are global distances: the connecting paths are not required to stay inside the unit square.

Define Hy = ∫01 h(x + iy) dx by L² limits of smooth mollifications, and let q be the lower median of miny∈[0,1] Hy. The proposed result states that q < 0 and (Dhγ(0, 1) − 1)/γ → (H0 − q)/2 in probability as γ ↓ 0 on the common field probability space.

Consequently the limiting distribution is Gaussian with mean −q/2 and variance 3/8. The point-distance median mγ = med Dhγ(0, 1) also satisfies mγ = 1 − (q/2)γ + o(γ). Thus the first-order normalization correction comes from minimizing horizontal line integrals across the square.

06

Two-Thirds Geodesic Deviation

Result

Let h be a whole-plane Gaussian free field with h1(0) = 0, and let Pγ be its γ-LQG geodesic from 0 to 1, regarded as an unparametrized curve. Set Mγ = maxz∈Pγ dist(z, [0, 1]), the largest Euclidean distance from the straight segment. This quantity is unchanged by multiplying the metric by a positive factor.

There are deterministic C < ∞ and γ0 > 0 such that 𝔼Mγ ≤ Cγ2/3 for 0 < γ < γ0, and ℙ[Mγ ≤ aγ2/3] ≤ Ca3/5 uniformly over 0 < a ≤ 1 and 0 < γ < γ0. For every A ≥ 1, the sharper upper-tail statement is limsupγ↓0 ℙ[Mγ > Aγ2/3] ≤ C(A−3 + A−4).

Consequently Mγ/γ2/3 is tight and uniformly bounded away from zero in probability as γ ↓ 0. Every subsequential weak limit is finite and strictly positive almost surely, and the inverse moments of orders 0 < r < 3/5 are uniformly bounded for 0 < γ < γ0. The theorem does not assert convergence to a unique law or exclude a positive deterministic subsequential limit.

Tightness of Small-Parameter Geodesics

Result

Let h be a whole-plane Gaussian free field with h1(0) = 0, and let Pγ be the almost surely unique γ-LQG geodesic from 0 to 1. Parametrize it on [0, 1] at constant metric speed, so Dhγ(0, Pγ(t)) = tDhγ(0, 1). Multiplying the metric by a positive constant does not change this parametrized path.

The family Xγ(t) = γ−2/3(Pγ(t) − t) is tight in C([0, 1], ℝ²), with its uniform topology, as γ ↓ 0. Moreover, supt∈[0,1]|Re Pγ(t) − t|/γ2/3 → 0 in probability, so any subsequential limit has zero horizontal component. The functional statement includes control of the modulus of continuity and uses the constant-speed parametrization; it does not identify a unique limiting law.

The proof imports the scalar mean bound, clock geometry, and frequency-localized estimate from the two-thirds-deviation note. Its additional conclusion is tightness of the entire rescaled path, beyond tightness of its maximum deviation.

Conformal Covariance of Supercritical LQG

Result

Fix ξ > ξcrit with Q = Q(ξ) ∈ (0, 2), and use one fixed normalization of the canonical supercritical Liouville quantum gravity metric. Let U, V ⊂ ℂ be domains, let φ: U → V be a fixed deterministic conformal bijection, and let h be a Gaussian free field plus a continuous function on V, in the local sense required to define the metric. Here Dh(x, y; V) is the internal distance, obtained by minimizing metric length over paths contained in V; it may equal +∞.

Almost surely, simultaneously for every u, v ∈ U, Dh(φ(u), φ(v); V) = Dh∘φ+Q log|φ′|(u, v; U). The multiplicative constant is exactly one, and the equality includes singular endpoints and infinite distances. The probability-one event may depend on φ; the statement is for each fixed map.

Higher-Dimensional LFPP Exponents

Result

Fix a dimension d ≥ 2 and normalize the whole-space log-correlated Gaussian field to have covariance singularity log(1/|x − y|). Let HN be its smooth white-noise cutoff from scales 2−N to 1, using a fixed smooth compactly supported radial kernel as specified in the note, so Var HN(x) = N log 2. Let aN(ξ) be the median of infP:0→e₁, P⊂(−2,2)d ∫PeξHN(z)|dz|, where e₁ is the first coordinate unit vector and the infimum is over rectifiable paths. Define λd(ξ) = −limN→∞ log aN(ξ)/(N log 2) = 1 − ξQd(ξ).

For each fixed d there are constants cd, Cd > 0 and ξd > 0 such that cdξ4/3/log(1/ξ) ≤ λd(ξ) ≤ Cdξ4/3 whenever 0 < ξ < ξd. In particular, log λd(ξ)/log ξ → 4/3. The constants and the small-parameter threshold may depend on d.

Under the metric-dimension correspondence Qd(ξ) = d/γ + γ/2 and ξ = γ/dγ, the same type of bounds holds for dγ − d: c′dγ4/3/log(1/γ) ≤ dγ − d ≤ C′dγ4/3 for sufficiently small γ. These are logarithmic-exponent conclusions; the note does not identify a leading coefficient or establish convergence of λd(ξ)/ξ4/3.

An Axiomatic Metric in Higher Dimensions

Result

Proposed theorem. Fix d ≥ 3 and ξ > 0 such that the continuum LFPP distance exponent satisfies Q = Q(ξ) > √(2d). The underlying field is the whole-space log-correlated Gaussian field on ℝd, with covariance kernel log(1/|x − y|) modulo constants. The proposed result constructs a measurable assignment h ↦ Dh of finite metrics inducing the Euclidean topology, defined for this field plus continuous functions.

The assignment satisfies four strong axioms: Dh is a length metric; its internal metric in each deterministic open set U is determined by h restricted to U; adding a continuous f reweights path length by eξf; and, for each fixed r > 0 and z ∈ ℝd, Dh(ru + z, rv + z) = Dh(r·+z)+Q log r(u, v) almost surely for all u, v.

Any two assignments satisfying these axioms at the same parameters are proposed to differ by a deterministic positive factor, simultaneously for all endpoint pairs and continuous field perturbations. For the continuum approximation kernels specified in the manuscript, the proposed result also gives convergence in probability of normalized LFPP metrics, locally uniformly, to this assignment. The approximation assertion is restricted to those kernels.

Sphere Averages in Odd Dimensions

Result

Let h be the whole-space log-correlated Gaussian field on ℝd, with covariance kernel log(1/|x − y|) modulo an additive constant, and let hr(0) denote its spherical average. For odd d = 2m + 1 ≥ 3, set St = he−t(0) − h1(0), t ∈ ℝ. As a continuous process, S has the same law as ∫0∞ qd(u)(Bt−u − B−u) du, where B is standard two-sided Brownian motion and qd(u) = (d − 2)e−2u(1 − e−2u)(d−4)/2 for u > 0.

The process has a modification locally in Cm−1,α for every 0 < α < 1. The increment variance of its (m − 1)st derivative is comparable to r² log(1/|r|) as r → 0, uniformly in the base time. An mth derivative does not exist in the mean-square sense.

The note also computes the exact spectrum of the generalized derivative of S. In odd dimensions this spectrum is nonrational, excluding a finite-dimensional linear Gaussian state-space realization; an explicit time-homogeneous Markov representation is instead constructed using an infinite-dimensional memory profile.

Quantum Cones in Odd Dimensions

Result

Let d ≥ 3 be odd, let 0 < γ < √(2d), and fix β < Q = d/γ + γ/2. For a whole-space log-correlated Gaussian field h on ℝd, normalized by h1(0) = 0, write St = he−t(0). For b > 0, define σb = inf{t ∈ ℝ : St − (Q − β)t = −b} and Sb,s = Ss+σb − Sσb.

For each fixed T ∈ ℝ, the law of (Sb,s)s≥T converges in total variation as b → ∞. These limits are compatible as T varies and define a continuous process S∞ on ℝ with S∞,0 = 0. The β-quantum cone is obtained by taking spherical averages S∞,s + βs and adding an independent copy of the field’s projection with zero average on every centered sphere.

More precisely, set h(b)(x) = h(e−σbx) − β log|e−σbx| − Qσb + b. On every fixed ball B(0, R), the law of h(b), viewed as a random distribution, converges in total variation to the cone field normalized to have unit-sphere average zero. The same local-limit statement holds about any fixed deterministic center after translating and normalizing the original field there.

Mesh-Only Convergence for Orthogonal Tilings

Result

Let d ≥ 2 and let D ⊂ ℝd be open. Consider locally finite orthogonal tilings of D by closed convex polytopes Pv with marked points v in their interiors: cells are joined across shared facets, and the line between their marked points is perpendicular to the shared facet. Give each edge the conductance a(v,w) = vold−1(Pv ∩ Pw)/|v−w|. Assume only that the maximum tile diameter εn tends to zero, with no additional cell-shape or cell-volume alternative.

Fix a bounded open U with closure compactly contained in D, and a fixed bounded open neighborhood K of the closure of U with closure compactly contained in D. Let h be harmonic and C2 on a neighborhood of the closure of K. For sufficiently large n, there is a unique discrete harmonic function hn on the vertices in U, with boundary values h at adjacent vertices outside U. The full note proves uniform convergence to h. The recovered supplement sharpens its quantitative bound to maxv∈Vₙ∩U|hn(v)−h(v)| ≤ Cd,U,K‖h‖C²(K) εn4/(d+4). Thus the planar exponent is 2/3. The 17-page note itself prints the earlier exponent 4/(3d+8); the three-page supplement supplies the sharper argument using estimates proved in that note.

The mesh-only approximation also verifies the harmonic-approximation input used for uniform convergence of harmonic measure on balls and convergence of the stopped canonical walk, whose transition probabilities are proportional to a(v,w). These consequences retain the remaining domain hypotheses of the cited Bou-Rabee–Gwynne results; walk convergence is to Brownian motion modulo time parametrization. The exponent 2/3 bounds the harmonic-extension error and is not a rate of convergence for a parametrized random walk. The full note and supplement are complementary: the supplement does not replace the analytic estimates or the walk-convergence argument.

Sphere Packings and Tangency-Polytope Tilings

Result

Let d ∈ {2, 3, 4}, let Ω ⊂ ℝd be any nonempty open set, and fix ε > 0. There is a countably infinite family of closed balls B(v,rv) ⊂ Ω with pairwise disjoint interiors and 0 < rv ≤ ε. For each ball, intersect the halfspaces bounded by its common tangent hyperplanes with all tangent balls, choosing the halfspace containing B(v,rv). The resulting tangency polytope Pv is bounded, and the polytopes form a locally finite, face-to-face tiling of exactly Ω.

Two balls are tangent if and only if their polytopes share a facet, and that facet lies in their common tangent hyperplane. Consequently, joining the ball centers across shared facets gives an orthogonal tiling graph. In dimensions 2, 3, and 4 respectively, each tile has at most 6, 12, and 24 facets, and B(v,rv) ⊂ Pv ⊂ B(v,Cdrv), where C2 = 2, C3 = √10, and C4 = 3√2 + 2.

No boundedness, connectedness, or boundary-regularity assumption on Ω is required. Local finiteness is relative to Ω, so accumulation at its boundary is allowed. Exact coverage is by the tangency polytopes; the balls need not cover Ω. The three PDFs give separate constructions in the three dimensions. The four-dimensional construction includes an exact-integer computer-assisted interpolation step and its complete checker.

Reflected Random Walks with Spatial Singularities

Result

Consider a random countable collection of compact connected planar cells with nonempty interiors, disjoint interiors, and boundaries of zero area. Adjacent cells are distinct and intersect, their adjacency graph is connected and locally finite, and each adjacent pair H,K has a symmetric conductance c(H,K) > 0. The singular-set version assumes a closed set S with one-dimensional Hausdorff measure ℋ1(S) = 0, coverage of ℂ ∖ S, spatial local finiteness off S, and connectedness of the induced cell graph along every horizontal or vertical segment avoiding S. The whole-plane version instead requires coverage of all ℂ and connectedness along every segment on almost every horizontal and vertical line; it allows spatial accumulation without a closed ℋ1-null witness. These are distinct geometric hypotheses.

In either version, assume mass transport modulo translations and positive dilations and ergodicity under these similarities. Write aH = Area(H), π(H) = ∑K∼Hc(H,K), and π*(H) = ∑K∼Hc(H,K)−1. If H0 is the almost surely unique cell whose interior contains the origin, the required moment is 𝔼[(diam(H0)2/aH₀)(π(H0) + π*(H0))] < ∞. The singular-set paper also specifies its geometric environment topology and a measurable full-probability class admitting the required witnesses; it derives graph local finiteness and the null-boundary property from its hypotheses.

Conditionally on almost every environment, the canonical walk reflected off infinity has a conservative area clock, with transition rate c(H,K)/aH and mean holding time aH/π(H). Choose representatives inside the cells, interpolate ordinary jumps, and extend the spatial path through reflection times as in the construction. From every fixed starting cell, (εZt/ε²)t≥0 converges in law, locally uniformly in time as ε ↓ 0, to Brownian motion with a deterministic finite positive-definite covariance matrix Σ. Assigning the exact holding time aH/π(H) at every visit gives the same limit and the same Σ. Neither manuscript asserts uniformity over starting cells chosen as a function of ε.
